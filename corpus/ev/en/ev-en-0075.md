# Should you buy an electric car in Georgia in 2026? A buyer's guide

## The case for

- **Running costs.** Charging at home costs a fraction of petrol. Maintenance is cheaper too.
- **Taxes.** Battery electric cars pay no excise tax, and imports are exempt from VAT until the end of 2026.
- **Charging.** The public network has grown to more than 500 charging points.
- **Clean electricity.** Most of Georgia's electricity comes from hydropower.

## The case against

- **Depreciation.** Used electric cars lost 18% of their value in 2025.
- **Charging access.** Many apartment dwellers have no place to charge at home.
- **Repairs and warranty.** Most cars are repaired auction imports without a manufacturer warranty.
- **Uncertainty.** The VAT exemption ends on 31 December 2026 unless it is extended.

## Our verdict

If you can charge at home and drive mostly in and around Tbilisi, an electric car makes financial sense. If you depend on public fast charging, do the math carefully.
